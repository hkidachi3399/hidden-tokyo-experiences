Heirinji Temple in Autumn: How to Get There from Ikebukuro

Heirinji is a working Zen temple in Niiza, just over the Tokyo border in Saitama. The temple buildings are modest. What people come for is the forest around them: about 106 acres of woodland designated a National Natural Monument, with maples that turn red from mid-November.

When to go

The usual window for autumn color is mid-November to early December, and the last week of November is a safe bet. It is popular at the peak, so aim for the morning, when the paths are calmer.

How to get there from Ikebukuro

There are two common routes, and both end with a bus of 15 to 20 minutes to the Heirinji bus stop, right by the temple:

  • Seibu Ikebukuro Line to Hibarigaoka Station, then a bus from the north side of the station.
  • Tobu Tojo Line to Shiki Station, then a bus from the south exit.

Buses do not run as often as trains, so check the timetable before you leave.

Hours and entry

  • Admission from 9:00 until 15:30. The grounds close at 16:00, so do not arrive late.
  • Entry is 500 yen for junior high school students and older, and 200 yen for elementary school students. Cards are not accepted.
  • The temple is closed in February for maintenance and on December 31. Check the official site for any weather closures.

What you see at the gate

The first thing is a thatched two-story outer gate, with a stone marker beside it naming the temple forest a Natural Monument. Past it, the inner gate carries a plaque that reads "Heirinji Senmon Dojo", a training hall for monks, and a gravel path runs between cedars and maples behind a low wooden-post fence. When I last walked it, I noticed one old wooden door with a bright red SECOM security sticker on it: a working temple, not a museum.

Next door is Suisoku-ken no Mori, a separate garden with a thatched house and a pond, about 100 meters from the temple entrance. It is easy to mix the two up, so check the signs.

How long to plan

The walking path through the grounds is about 2.5 km and takes around an hour and a half at a relaxed pace.
