Akihabara or Toshima: Where to Go for the Real Birthplace of Manga

Most visitors to Tokyo who love manga head to Akihabara. It is worth a visit, but if your question is "where did modern manga actually grow up?", the answer is a calm, old-fashioned neighborhood in Toshima ward, about 15 minutes by train from Ikebukuro.

What each place is for

Akihabara is a shopping and fan culture district. It started as an electronics town and grew into the center of anime, games and collectibles. It is loud and a little rough around the edges, and that is part of the fun. Go there to buy, browse and feel the scale of fandom today.

Minami-Nagasaki, Toshima is where the people behind the stories lived. In the 1950s, a wooden apartment building called Tokiwaso housed young artists, including Osamu Tezuka (Astro Boy), the two artists behind Doraemon, Shotaro Ishinomori (Cyborg 009, Kamen Rider) and Fujio Akatsuka. Go there to understand how the work was made.

About the museum itself

The original building was torn down in 1982. A near-life-size replica opened in 2020 in a small park, about 200 meters from the original site. You take off your shoes and walk the corridors where rooms are set up as the artists lived. It is small, so go for the atmosphere and the stories, not for a big collection.

My suggestion

Do both, on different days. If you only have one morning and you already know a little about Tezuka or Doraemon, choose Toshima: it gives you the stories behind what you will later see in the shops.

I guide small groups to the Tokiwaso area on a walking tour that ends with a local lunch. Before you go, check the museum's official site for opening days, reservations and any special exhibition fee.
