Totoro Forest Tour: Walk the Sayama Hills, 40 Minutes from Tokyo

The Sayama Hills straddle the border between Tokyo and Saitama: roughly 3,500 hectares of woodland, tea fields and hidden ponds that survived the city growing around them. They are often linked to the landscape that inspired My Neighbor Totoro, and the local conservation movement protecting the woods is known as the Totoro no Furusato (Totoro's Homeland) Fund.

This half-day walk, guided by a local, takes you through the forest to Lake Sayama, with a stop for a cup of tea. Groups are limited to four guests.

What the walk feels like

Within about ten minutes of leaving the station the convenience stores give way to tea fields, and the trail dips into forest where the temperature drops a few degrees. On a weekday morning you may share the hillside with very few people. Lake Sayama appears suddenly through the trees, free of tour buses.

The tea

These hills produce Sayama tea, one of Japan's classic tea regions, known for a deep, roasted sweetness. On the walk you stop for a cup poured by a local farmer.

Why go with a guide

There is no gate, no gift shop and little English signage, so it helps to have someone who knows the trails, the tea farmers and which paths are best after rain. Read the full story in Totoro's Forest Is Real.
