Tokyo in December: Crisp Blue Skies, Clear Fuji Views, and New-Season Sake in Hanno

Guests who visit in December usually arrive expecting gray, wet winter weather. What they get instead, most days, is the opposite: cold, dry air and a sky so clear that Mt. Fuji shows up on the horizon from rooftops and train platforms all over the city. I've lived in Tokyo my whole life, and December is the month I'd pick for photographs. The light is low, sharp and golden all day, and the haze that hides Fuji for most of the summer is gone.

It is also the month when Tokyo gets busy in a very particular way. The city center fills with illuminations, year-end parties and shoppers, while the quiet towns an hour west of Ikebukuro stay just as calm as they are the rest of the year. That gap is what this guide is about.

What December weather actually feels like

Expect daytime highs of around 12°C and nights that drop to a few degrees above freezing. It rarely rains, and snow in central Tokyo before January is unusual. The catch is the short days: the sun sets around 4:30pm, and the temperature falls quickly once it does. Plan outdoor walks for the morning and early afternoon, and bring a warm layer, gloves and a hat for the walk back to the station. Indoors, trains and shops are well heated, so layers you can take off work better than one heavy coat.

Why December is the start of sake season

Winter is when Japanese sake breweries do most of their brewing. The cold keeps fermentation slow and clean, and from around December the first freshly pressed sake of the new season starts to appear. It is lively, young and very different from the bottles you find in airport shops.

That makes December a good month for my Hanno forest, fermentation park and sake tour. Hanno is about an hour from Ikebukuro on a direct line, the brewery town sits right against the mountains, and the forest walk is at its best in clear, cool weather. You get a 500-year-old Zen temple garden, a fermentation theme park and a sake tasting in one easy day, with no crowds.

Other quiet December days west of the city

Totoro's forest in the Sayama Hills. Once the leaves fall, the woods open up and you can see much farther along the trails. On a clear morning it is one of the most peaceful walks near Tokyo. My guide to Totoro Forest in the Sayama Hills covers how it works.

Heirinji Zen temple. In a late year, the autumn colors at Heirinji can last into the first days of December. Even after they are gone, the moss, the bamboo and the silence of a working Zen monastery are worth the trip. See my Heirinji guide.

A hot bath at the end of a cold walk. The riverside walk that ends at Spa Japon, which I wrote about in my November guide, makes even more sense when it is properly cold outside.

Year-end closures: plan around December 29 to January 3

The last few days of the year are Japan's biggest holiday. Many museums, small shops and restaurants close from around December 29 until January 3, and trains run on holiday schedules. If your trip covers those dates, check opening days before you plan anything, and book tours earlier than usual. On New Year's Eve, temples across Tokyo ring their bells late into the night, and on New Year's Day crowds line up for the first shrine visit of the year. It is worth seeing once, but it is the opposite of a quiet day out.

My short December checklist

Go west in the morning for the clearest Fuji views. Pack gloves for after sunset. Try new-season sake while you are here. Avoid booking anything you cannot change for December 29 to January 3 until you have checked it is open.
