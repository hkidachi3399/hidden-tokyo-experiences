Tokyo in September: Typhoon Season, Thin Crowds, and the Best Rainy-Day Tour

Every year around this time, I get the same message from guests planning a trip: "Should we avoid Tokyo in September because of typhoons?" It's a fair question, and the honest answer is a local's answer — yes, plan around the weather, but no, don't avoid the month. September is quietly one of the best times to be here, and almost nobody realizes it.

I live in this city year-round, and September has a personality all its own: the brutal August heat finally breaks, the summer festival crowds have gone home, school is back in session, and the autumn leaf-peeping rush hasn't started yet. What's left is a strange, comfortable gap — thinner queues at the places that matter, and a city that feels like it belongs to the people who actually live here again.

The typhoon question, answered honestly

September and early October sit inside Japan's typhoon season, and it's real — a storm can shut down trains, cancel outdoor plans, and occasionally close museums for a day. But most of the month is ordinary, often beautifully so: warm, humid, occasionally rainy, with none of August's punishing heat. The practical move isn't to avoid the month, it's to build a little flexibility into your itinerary — keep an eye on the Japan Meteorological Agency's forecast a few days out, and have one indoor backup planned in case a storm rolls through while you're here.

This is exactly the kind of local knowledge that guidebooks skip and that changes a trip. A typhoon day in Tokyo doesn't have to be a lost day — it just needs a different plan.

The tour that saves a rainy day

Most of my tours are outdoor forest walks, temple grounds and riverside strolls — glorious on a clear day, less fun in a downpour. There's one exception, and this week I'm featuring it for exactly that reason: the Birthplace of Manga & Anime Guided Museum Walk in Minami-Nagasaki.

The walk mixes an indoor museum visit — the faithfully rebuilt Tokiwaso apartment where Tezuka, the Doraemon duo and Kamen Rider's creator all lived — with an easy, mostly-covered neighborhood stroll. If a September shower catches us mid-tour, we simply spend longer inside; nobody's day is ruined. It's the one tour on my roster I recommend without hesitation when the forecast looks uncertain.

What September actually looks like on the ground

Practically speaking: pack a light rain jacket over an umbrella (Tokyo wind turns umbrellas inside out more than you'd think), wear shoes that dry fast, and build one buffer day into a multi-day itinerary if you can. Trains keep running through all but the worst storms, so a typhoon rarely traps you — it just reshuffles the day. On the clear days, which are most of them, September light is gentler than August's glare, and early evenings are genuinely pleasant for the first time since June.

If you're weighing when to book a West Tokyo walking tour, September rewards flexibility more than any other month: pick a date, watch the week-out forecast, and keep the manga walk in your back pocket as the plan that survives bad weather.
