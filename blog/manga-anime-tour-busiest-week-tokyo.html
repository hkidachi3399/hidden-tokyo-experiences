Three Bookings in One Week: What Tokyo's Manga Pilgrims Taught Me

I keep a simple spreadsheet of who's booked which tour and when — nothing fancy, just enough to plan my weekends around my day job in Tokyo. This week, scrolling through it, one line jumped out: the Birthplace of Manga & Anime walk had been booked three times in seven days. Every other tour on my roster gets maybe one group a week, if that. This one had moved at a completely different speed, and I wanted to understand why.

Three guests, three very different trips

The first was Julio, visiting from Mexico. He picked the Extended route — the version that keeps walking past Tokiwaso all the way to the old Toei Animation studio grounds, birthplace of Dragon Ball, Sailor Moon, One Piece and Pretty Cure. He grinned through most of the walk. "Anime is huge back home," he told me, more than once, like he still couldn't quite believe he was standing where it started. A few hours later that same day, a second guest booked the Standard route through a different platform entirely — no connection to Julio, just two strangers independently deciding the same Tuesday was the day to see Tokiwaso. By the end of the week, a third guest had booked for the following Saturday.

None of these three knew about each other. They found the tour through different search terms, different platforms, different corners of the internet. And yet all three ended up walking the same quiet street in Toshima Ward, for what amounts to the same reason.

Why this tour, out of everything I guide

I guide six other tours — a cycling route around Lake Tama, a farm visit in Kiyose, a Zen temple walk, a sake brewery day in Hanno. All of them get good reviews. None of them get booked three times in a week. The difference, as far as I can tell, isn't marketing. It's that Tokiwaso isn't a place I'm trying to convince anyone is interesting. It already is. Osamu Tezuka, the Fujiko Fujio duo, Shotaro Ishinomori — they lived in that building, poor and twenty-something, trading pages and instant ramen, and the manga and anime that came out of those rooms now reaches almost every country on earth. I'm not selling an experience so much as pointing at something that was already true before I showed up.

What surprises me every time is how far that pull reaches. A guest from Mexico, a guest from an English-speaking country I never got to ask about directly, a third guest I haven't met yet as I write this — all converging on the same unremarkable residential block because of characters they grew up with. It's the clearest reminder I get, in either of my two jobs, that the things people actually love don't respect borders.

Standard or Extended — the choice is part of the experience

For anyone weighing the same decision Julio and the second guest made: the Standard route covers the Tokiwaso Manga Museum and a stop at Matsuba, the ramen shop the artists actually ate at, in about two hours. The Extended route adds the walk out to the former Toei Animation Studio grounds — the birthplace of some of the biggest anime franchises in the world — and runs closer to two and a half. Neither is the "better" option; it depends on whether you came for the manga side of the story, the anime side, or both.

What I'm taking from this week

Running tours on weekends while working a full-time job the rest of the week means I don't get to overthink every booking — I notice patterns after the fact, usually while trying to plan next week's schedule. This week's pattern was a good one to notice: the quietest street on my roster is, for a very specific and very global audience, the whole point of the trip.
