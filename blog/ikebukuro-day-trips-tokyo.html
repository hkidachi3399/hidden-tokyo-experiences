Four Quiet Day Trips from Ikebukuro (None of Them Are Kamakura or Nikko)

Ask most first-time visitors where to go for a break from Tokyo and you will hear the same two names: Kamakura and Nikko. Both are lovely, and both come with a long train ride, a lot of other people, and a day that is mostly spent getting there and back. I live on the western side of the city, and I keep sending guests the other way — out along the lines that leave from Ikebukuro, where within an hour the buildings thin out and the Tokyo that locals actually weekend in begins.

Here are four of those escapes. None needs more than about an hour each way, none needs a rental car, and all of them work as a relaxed half day.

1. A Zen monastery in the woods (about 30 minutes)

Heirinji is a working Zen temple with a 500-year history, set inside a wooded precinct that feels far older and further away than its location suggests. The approach is a long path through cedar and oak, and on a weekday morning you may share it with a few monks and almost no one else. It is the closest I can get a guest to a "quiet Japan" moment without leaving the Tokyo area. Our Heirinji Monks' Path & Forest Meditation Walk is built around exactly that stillness, and you can read the longer story in The Zen Monastery in the Woods.

2. Totoro's forest in the Sayama Hills (about 40 minutes)

The hills that inspired so many of Japan's most loved animated forests are real, walkable, and surprisingly empty. Misty cedar paths, small shrines tucked off the trail, and a lake at the end of the walk make this a good pick for anyone who grew up with those films, and an equally good one for people who simply want to be among trees. The full write-up is in Totoro's Forest Is Real.

3. A working farm and a local market in Kiyose

This is the least "sightseeing" of the four, and often the one guests talk about longest. You visit a real farm, see what is growing in the Tokyo suburbs in the current season, stop at the local produce market, and finish with a handmade soba lunch. If you are curious how ordinary people in Tokyo eat and shop, start with Tokyo's Most Unscripted Morning.

4. Sake and fermentation in Hanno (about one hour)

Hanno is the furthest of the four, and it rewards the extra distance: a Zen temple garden, a small theme park devoted to fermentation, and a tasting at a brewery that uses mountain spring water. It is a full-day outing rather than a quick half day, but it is still comfortably close to the city. The longer account is in One Hour from Shinjuku, Zero Tourists.

How to choose

If you want silence, pick Heirinji. If you want scenery and a walk, pick the Sayama Hills. If you want to meet people and eat well, pick Kiyose. If you want a proper day out with a drink at the end, pick Hanno. If you are still undecided, the first-timer's guide to choosing a tour lays out all of them side by side.

All of our tours are small-group, with a maximum of four guests, so there is room to ask questions, change pace and actually talk to your guide.
